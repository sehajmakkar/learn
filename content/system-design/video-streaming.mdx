## The problem: a cover video on every connection

Snip Bio lets creators add a 60-second video to their profile. The upload is a 1080p file from a phone, around 150
MB. A visitor on fast Wi-Fi should see it sharp; one on a train with a weak signal should see it play without
stalling, just blurrier; and if the signal improves halfway through, the picture should get sharper without
restarting.

Downloading one file can't do that. **Adaptive bitrate streaming** can.

## 1. Transcode into a ladder

A worker (<L id="07.05" />) **transcodes** the upload into several versions at different resolutions and bitrates:
a **ladder**. Apple's HLS authoring specification gives one possible ladder for 16:9 H.264 video, for example:

| Resolution | Bitrate (kbit/s) |
| --- | --- |
| 416 × 234 | 145 |
| 640 × 360 | 365 |
| 960 × 540 | 2,000 |
| 1280 × 720 | 3,000 to 4,500 |
| 1920 × 1080 | 6,000 to 7,800 |

Transcoding is the heavy step: minutes of CPU per minute of video per rung. It parallelises well by splitting the
video into pieces, transcoding them on many workers and joining the results, so even long videos are ready quickly.

## 2. Cut it into segments

Each version is cut into short **segments**, a few seconds each, stored as separate files. Apple's specification
says target durations should be 6 seconds, and that key frames (frames that decode without reference to earlier
ones) should be present every two seconds.

Segments are what make switching possible: the player can fetch segment 5 at 2,000 kbit/s and segment 6 at 6,000
kbit/s. That only works if every rung's segments start at the same moments, on a keyframe, which is why keyframe
placement is aligned across the ladder.

## 3. Describe it in playlists

With HLS (HTTP Live Streaming, RFC 8216), a **master playlist** lists the variants, and each variant has a **media
playlist** listing its segments:

```
#EXTM3U
#EXT-X-STREAM-INF:BANDWIDTH=545000,RESOLUTION=640x360
360p/index.m3u8
#EXT-X-STREAM-INF:BANDWIDTH=2400000,RESOLUTION=960x540
540p/index.m3u8
#EXT-X-STREAM-INF:BANDWIDTH=7000000,RESOLUTION=1920x1080
1080p/index.m3u8
```

```
#EXTM3U
#EXT-X-VERSION:3
#EXT-X-TARGETDURATION:6
#EXT-X-MEDIA-SEQUENCE:0
#EXTINF:6.0,
seg-000.ts
#EXTINF:6.0,
seg-001.ts
#EXT-X-ENDLIST
```

`BANDWIDTH` is the variant's peak segment bitrate in bits per second. The **player** measures how fast segments
arrive and how much video it has buffered, and picks the variant for each next segment. RFC 8216 deliberately leaves
that switching algorithm to clients. DASH is the other widely used format; the idea is the same.

## 4. Serve it from the CDN

Every segment is a small, immutable file, which is exactly what CDNs are best at (<L id="02.05" />): cache them for a
long time, and the origin sees almost nothing after the first viewers. A popular video's segments are served from
edges near viewers; the playlists, tiny and cacheable too, point at them.

## What it costs

- **Storage multiplies**: every rung is a full copy of the video. A short tail of rarely watched videos can skip the
  top rungs, generated only if anyone watches.
- **Encoding time** before the video is ready: show "processing" in the meantime, and transcode the lowest rung first
  so playback can start soon.
- **Segment length trade-off**: shorter segments switch quality and start faster but mean more requests and less
  efficient compression; longer ones are the reverse.

<Analogy>
  **Delivering a long letter by many postcards.** Instead of one heavy envelope, the letter is copied in large, medium and small handwriting and split into numbered postcards. If the post is slow, the reader asks for the next card in small writing; when it speeds up, in large writing. The story never stops, it just gets easier or harder to read.
</Analogy>

<Mistakes>
  <ul>
    <li>**Serving one big file.** It can't adapt to the connection. Use segmented adaptive streaming.</li>
    <li>**Unaligned keyframes across rungs.** Switching breaks. Force a fixed keyframe interval for every rung.</li>
    <li>**Encoding every rung for every video.** Rarely watched videos can skip expensive top rungs.</li>
    <li>**Waiting for the whole ladder.** Publish the lowest rung first so playback can start.</li>
  </ul>
</Mistakes>

## Recap

<Recap>
  <ul>
    <li>Transcode each upload into a ladder of resolutions and bitrates.</li>
    <li>Cut every rung into short segments, around 6 seconds, starting on aligned keyframes.</li>
    <li>Playlists list variants and segments; the player measures throughput and picks each next segment's quality.</li>
    <li>Segments are small immutable files, ideal for CDN caching; storage and encoding are the main costs.</li>
  </ul>
</Recap>
