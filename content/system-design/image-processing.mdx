## The problem: a 12 MB photo on a phone

Snip Bio creators upload a profile photo and a cover image, often straight from a phone camera: 12 MB, 4,000 pixels
wide, with GPS coordinates in its metadata. Visitors view profiles on phones over mobile data, where the avatar is 96
pixels across. Serving the original would mean downloading 12 MB to show an image the size of a thumbnail, and
publishing where the creator lives.

## What one upload becomes

- **Sizes**: an avatar at 96, 192 and 384 pixels; a cover at 640, 1,280 and 2,560. Larger sizes cover high-density
  screens.
- **Formats**: modern formats like WebP and AVIF are much smaller than JPEG for the same quality, for browsers that
  support them, with JPEG as the fallback.
- **Stripped metadata**: remove EXIF data (camera, time, GPS location) from every served copy.
- **Fixed orientation**: apply the phone's rotation flag, so images don't appear sideways.

The original stays in object storage (<L id="09.04" />), untouched, so variants can always be regenerated.

## Make variants up front, or on demand?

**At upload**: a job (<L id="07.05" />) creates every variant and stores them. Reads are plain cached files. But
every upload pays for sizes that may never be requested, and adding a new size means reprocessing every image.

**On demand at the edge**: the image URL carries the transformation (`/img/avatar/4821.jpg?width=192&format=auto`), and
an image service at the CDN fetches the original, transforms it, and caches the result. Cloudflare, for example,
describes its transformations as optimising and caching remote images from any origin at the edge: on a miss it fetches
the original, applies the requested parameters such as width, format and quality, and caches the result. Nothing is
generated until someone asks for it.

Snip does both: avatar sizes are generated at upload (they're on every profile view), and covers are transformed on
demand.

## Don't let parameters explode the cache

On-demand transforms put parameters in the URL, so every distinct parameter set is a separate cache entry and a
separate, CPU-heavy transform (<L id="06.07" />). Left open, `width=191`, `width=192`, `width=193` … are all different,
and anyone can make the service resize images all day. So:

- **Allow a fixed set of sizes** (snap requests to the nearest allowed width) and qualities.
- **Sign transformation URLs**, or only accept them from your own pages, so others can't generate arbitrary work.
- **Cache the transformed result for a long time**: originals don't change in place, and a new upload gets a new key.

## Let the browser pick

The page offers the sizes and lets the browser choose, based on how large the image will appear and the screen's pixel
density:

```html
<img
  src="/img/cover/4821.jpg?width=1280"
  srcset="/img/cover/4821.jpg?width=640 640w,
          /img/cover/4821.jpg?width=1280 1280w,
          /img/cover/4821.jpg?width=2560 2560w"
  sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 600px"
  alt="Cover image"
  width="1280" height="480" />
```

`srcset` lists the candidates with their real widths; `sizes` says how wide the image will be on screen. A phone 375
pixels wide at 3× density needs about 1,125 pixels, so it picks the 1,280 version, not the 2,560. The `width` and
`height` attributes reserve space so the page doesn't jump as images load.

<Analogy>
  **A photo lab.** You hand in one negative (the original) and keep it safe. Prints are made in the sizes people actually order, some in advance for the shop window and others on request, from a fixed list of sizes so the lab isn't asked for 191 slightly different ones. And no print shows what's written on the back of the negative.
</Analogy>

<Mistakes>
  <ul>
    <li>**Serving originals.** Generate sized, modern-format variants.</li>
    <li>**Keeping EXIF on served images.** Strip metadata; GPS coordinates are personal data.</li>
    <li>**Arbitrary transform parameters.** Allow a fixed set, and sign URLs.</li>
    <li>**One size for every screen.** Use srcset and sizes so each device downloads what it needs.</li>
  </ul>
</Mistakes>

## Recap

<Recap>
  <ul>
    <li>Keep the original; serve variants in several sizes and modern formats, with metadata stripped.</li>
    <li>Generate common sizes at upload; transform the rest on demand at the edge, cached.</li>
    <li>Constrain transform parameters to a fixed set, and sign them, so caches work and abuse is bounded.</li>
    <li>Let the browser choose with srcset and sizes.</li>
  </ul>
</Recap>
