## Why estimate at all

A design for 10 requests a second and one for 100,000 look nothing alike. The first fits on one machine; the second
needs <Term t="cache">caches</Term>, many servers and probably split-up data. A **back-of-the-envelope estimate** is a two-minute calculation
that tells you which world you're in.

The rule for interviews and real work alike: **only estimate what changes a decision.** If the answer to "how much
storage?" is "a few hundred gigabytes", that settles it (one disk) and you move on. Long tables of numbers that
nobody uses are wasted time.

<Analogy>
  **Planning a party.** You don't count every crisp. You estimate guests, roughly how much each eats, and whether that's one trip to the shop or a van. The goal is the decision (one trip or two), not the exact number.
</Analogy>

## The method

Five steps, each one a multiplication or division:

<Flow
  title="From users to machines"
  steps={[
    { label: "Actions per day", detail: "users × actions per user per day" },
    { label: "Per second", detail: "divide by 100,000 (seconds in a day, rounded)" },
    { label: "Peak", detail: "multiply by 2–3: traffic isn't flat" },
    { label: "Storage", detail: "records × size × how long you keep them" },
    { label: "Decide", detail: "does it fit one machine? which part doesn't?" },
  ]}
/>

Round hard at every step. 86,400 becomes 100,000; 333,000 becomes 300,000. You're after the right power of ten, not
the right digits.

## Worked example: Snip

From <L id="01.02" />: 10 million new links a month, and about 100 <Term t="redirect">redirects</Term> for every link created. Assume each
stored link (short code, long URL, owner, timestamps) takes about **500 bytes**, and we keep links for five years.

**Writes (creating links)**

- 10 million a month ÷ 30 days ≈ 330,000 a day
- 330,000 ÷ 100,000 seconds ≈ **3–4 writes per second**
- Peak at three times average: about **10 writes per second**

**Reads (redirects)**

- 100 × the write rate ≈ **350 reads per second**, about **1,000** at peak

**Storage**

- 10 million links a month × 12 months × 5 years = 600 million links
- 600 million × 500 bytes = **300 GB**

**Decide.** Ten writes a second, a thousand reads a second and 300 GB of data: one ordinary database server handles
that, with room to spare. Snip at this size doesn't need <Term t="sharding">sharding</Term>, a <Term t="message-queue">message queue</Term> or a fleet of caches. We'd still add
a second database copy so a single failure doesn't take redirects down (that's <Term t="availability">availability</Term>, from <L id="01.03" />),
but scale isn't the problem.

Now multiply the traffic by a hundred: a billion new links a month. That's about 350 writes and 35,000 reads a second,
and 30 TB over five years. Now the reads want a cache, and the data may need splitting across machines. **Same
system, different design, and the estimate is what told you.**

## Things people forget

- **Peaks.** Traffic follows the clock: evenings, launches, a link going viral. Design for peak, not average.
- **Copies.** If you keep three copies of every record for <Term t="durability">durability</Term>, storage is three times the raw number.
- **Indexes and overhead.** Databases store more than your raw rows. Adding 30–50% is a reasonable rough allowance.
- **Growth.** If traffic doubles each year, five years of data is mostly the last two years.

You don't need to apply all of these every time. Mention the ones that could change the decision.

<Mistakes>
  <ul>
    <li>**False precision.** "11,574.07 requests per second" wastes time and suggests you've misunderstood the point. Say "about 10,000, 30,000 at peak".</li>
    <li>**Estimating for the sake of it.** If a number won't change the design, skip it, or say "this is small enough not to matter".</li>
    <li>**Stopping at the number.** The estimate is only useful once you say what it means: "fits on one machine", "needs a cache", "needs splitting".</li>
  </ul>
</Mistakes>

## Recap

<Recap>
  <ul>
    <li>Estimate only what changes a decision, and round to powers of ten.</li>
    <li>Per day ÷ 100,000 ≈ per second; multiply by 2–3 for peak.</li>
    <li>Storage = records × size × retention, times copies.</li>
    <li>Snip at 10M links a month fits on one database server; at 100× it doesn't.</li>
  </ul>
</Recap>
