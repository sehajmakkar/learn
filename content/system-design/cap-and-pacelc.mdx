## The problem: the cable between regions is cut

A fibre fault cuts the link between Snip's Frankfurt and Virginia regions for twenty minutes. Both regions are up, and
users on each side can reach their own. An owner in Berlin edits `x7Kp`; Frankfurt can't tell Virginia. Snip has to
choose, right now:

- **Accept the edit** in Frankfurt. Virginia keeps redirecting to the old destination until the link heals: available,
  but not consistent.
- **Refuse the edit** until Virginia can be told. Everyone sees the same thing, but Berlin owners get errors for twenty
  minutes: consistent, but not available.

That forced choice is the CAP theorem.

## What CAP says, precisely

The everyday version, "consistency, availability, partition tolerance: pick two", causes most of the confusion. Martin
Kleppmann's reading of the formal version is the useful one:

- **C** means **linearizable** (<L id="08.02" />), not "consistent" in any looser sense.
- **A** means every request to a non-failing node gets a non-error response, eventually.
- **P** isn't really a choice. Networks partition whether you like it or not.

So CAP says: **when the network is partitioned, a system can't be both linearizable and fully available.** That's it.
Eric Brewer, who stated the conjecture, wrote twelve years later that the "2 of 3" formulation "was always misleading",
and that since partitions are rare there's little reason to give up C or A when the system isn't partitioned.

Three consequences people miss:

- **The choice is per operation, not per database.** Snip can stay available for redirects and refuse code claims in
  the same partition.
- **Most systems are neither "CP" nor "AP".** A single-leader database with async replicas isn't linearizable (replicas
  lag) and isn't available on the minority side of a split. Kleppmann's point is that these labels describe almost
  nothing real; describe the actual guarantees instead.
- **It says nothing about the 99.9% of the time with no partition.** That's where PACELC comes in.

## PACELC: the trade-off you pay every day

Daniel Abadi's extension adds the case that matters most often:

**If there is a Partition**, choose **Availability or Consistency**; **Else**, choose **Latency or Consistency**.

Even with a healthy network, keeping replicas consistent means waiting for them. A linearizable write in Snip waits
for the other region to confirm, about 100 ms across the Atlantic. Answering from the local replica is fast but may be
stale. Every replicated system makes this trade on every request, which is why it shapes designs far more than
partitions do.

| System (default settings) | During a partition | Normally | PACELC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dynamo, Cassandra, Riak | Availability | Latency | PA/EL |
| VoltDB/H-Store, Megastore | Consistency | Consistency | PC/EC |
| BigTable, HBase | Consistency | Consistency | PC/EC |

Systems with tunable settings move along this: raising read and write <Term t="quorum">quorums</Term> in Cassandra buys consistency with
latency (<L id="08.04" />).

## Snip, operation by operation

| Operation | Partition (PA or PC) | Normally (EL or EC) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Redirect | A: answer from the local region | L: local cache and replica | A redirect to a minutes-old destination beats an error |
| Edit a destination | A: accept locally, reconcile later | L: async copy to the other region | Edits are rare and per-owner; last edit wins, with versions (<L id="05.06" />) |
| Claim a custom code | C: refuse in the minority region | C: route to the code's home region | Two owners must never both get `summer` |
| Charge for Pro | C | C | Money |

<Analogy>
  **Two branches of a bank during a phone outage.** A customer at one branch wants to withdraw. The branch can pay out (available) and risk the account being emptied at the other branch too, or refuse until the line is back (consistent). And even on a normal day, phoning the other branch before every withdrawal is slower than trusting the local ledger: that everyday trade is the "else" in PACELC.
</Analogy>

<Mistakes>
  <ul>
    <li>**"Pick two of three."** P isn't optional; the choice is C or A, during partitions only.</li>
    <li>**Labelling whole databases CP or AP.** Choices are per operation, and most systems fit neither label.</li>
    <li>**Ignoring the everyday trade.** Latency versus consistency (PACELC's "else") matters far more often than partitions.</li>
    <li>**Using "consistency" loosely.** CAP's C is linearizability, not ACID's C.</li>
  </ul>
</Mistakes>

## Recap

<Recap>
  <ul>
    <li>CAP: during a network partition, a system can't be both linearizable and fully available.</li>
    <li>"Two of three" misleads: partitions aren't optional, they're rare, and the choice is per operation.</li>
    <li>PACELC adds the everyday trade: without a partition, consistency still costs latency.</li>
    <li>Describe real guarantees per operation instead of labelling systems CP or AP.</li>
  </ul>
</Recap>

## Interview questions

<InterviewCard
  q={{ q: "Explain the CAP theorem. What's commonly misunderstood about it?", level: "Mid", frequency: 3, importance: "Must-know", kind: "Concept" }}
  followUps={["What does PACELC add?", "Give an example of choosing differently for two operations in one system."]}
>
  <p>CAP says that when a network partition occurs, a distributed system must choose between consistency, meaning linearizability, and availability, meaning every request to a working node gets a non-error response. The common misunderstanding is "pick two of three": partitions aren't something you can opt out of, so the real choice is C or A while partitioned, and when there's no partition you can have both.</p>
  <p>Two more nuances: the choice can be made per operation, and many systems are neither strictly CP nor AP. PACELC extends CAP: if partitioned, choose availability or consistency; else choose latency or consistency, because keeping replicas in sync costs round trips even when the network is fine. For a URL shortener I'd keep redirects available and low-latency from local replicas, but make custom-code claims and payments consistent, refusing them in a minority partition.</p>
</InterviewCard>
